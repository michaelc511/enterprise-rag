Every SaaS company that holds customer data has the same two problems. It must prove that its vendors are safe, and it must prove to its customers that it is safe. Both jobs come down to reading contracts, audit reports and policies carefully, and that reading is still mostly done by hand. This series builds a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system that does that reading with citations, PII redaction and evals. The fictional company is OpsVault, an enterprise workflow platform.

1. Meet OpsVault

OpsVault is a fictional cloud SaaS and PaaS company modeled on enterprise workflow platforms. One platform runs a company's IT, HR, customer service, risk, finance and supply-chain workflows, with AI agents built into every product. Customers either buy ready-made products (SaaS) or build their own apps on the OpsVault Platform (PaaS).

OpsVault AIGenerative AI assistants and agents built into every product

Risk & CompliancePolicies, audits and vendor risk. This is where the RAG system lives.

OpsVault is held to SOC 2 Type II (control IDs like CC6.1, CC7.2, CC8.1), to SOX for finance-related vendors, and to PCI DSS wherever card data appears. It relies on a few critical vendors. Stratocore is the cloud host that runs the platform. Cortexa is the AI model provider that powers OpsVault AI. Harborline is a logistics partner.

Customer Zero. OpsVault runs every product on itself first and then sells what worked. This RAG system follows the same path. OpsVault's own procurement, security, GRC, legal and sales teams use it first. Then it ships to customers as an OpsVault AI skill inside Risk & Compliance.

2. The pain: two directions, one pile of documents

Inbound: "Is this vendor safe?"

OpsVault's vendor policy defines eight requirements, VM-1 to VM-8. Every critical vendor must meet them before signing and for the whole contract. GRC (governance, risk and compliance) checks each contract and SOC 2 report against those requirements and records every gap as a risk. Today that means an analyst opens three documents side by side and hunts for the right clause.

Outbound: "Prove that we're safe."

Customers send security questionnaires before they buy. The synthetic one from Brightwater asks things like "How often are encryption keys rotated?" and "Is our data used to train or improve any AI model, by you or by any subprocessor?" Sales can't close until security answers, and every answer has to match OpsVault's own policy and SOC 2 report exactly.

Why this is hard, and why plain chat with an LLM won't do

Pain pointWhat goes wrong today
PrecisionThe answer is one clause, such as "Section 12.2" or "$1,680,000". A close-enough paraphrase is wrong.
Cross-document reasoningThe real question spans the contract, the vendor's audit report and OpsVault's policy. An SOW can quietly override its MSA.
Fine print"No training on your data" in one section, then "notwithstanding Section 5.3…" in the next.
Sensitive contentContracts carry signatory names, emails, phone numbers, bank details, even live API keys. None of that may reach an index or an LLM.
Trust and auditAuditors and legal need to see where an answer came from and who asked what.
AI vendor riskAI providers are new critical vendors, and their training and retention terms are the newest kind of risk.
SpeedVendor onboarding and sales deals both stall waiting on reviews.

Stratocore (cloud host): the cross-document showcase

"The cloud vendor's MSA requires encryption at rest. Does their SOC 2 report confirm it, and does that meet OpsVault's policy?"

  • Contract: MSA §7.2 requires AES-256 and key rotation at least once every 12 months.
  • Audit report: SC-ENC-01 (AES-256) shows no exceptions. SC-ENC-02 (key rotation) has an exception noted: 2 of 25 sampled keys (8%) were last rotated 14 and 16 months before testing.
  • Policy: VM-2 requires rotation to be evidenced in the SOC 2 Type II report. So encryption at rest meets the policy, but key rotation is a finding to log in the risk register.
  • Hidden override: SOW-02 (Backup & DR) overrides MSA §10.1 for backup copies only. Backups are deleted at the end of a 35-day retention period, not within 30 days after export. A clause search inside the MSA alone would miss this.

And the content that must never leave

The same SOW contains a live integration key and named people. After the S5 redaction step, the index stores only typed placeholders:

During setup, the integration will use the temporary API key [API_KEY_1] issued by OpsVault.
Stratocore must replace this key with a vaulted credential before go-live ...

- OpsVault service owner: [PERSON_1], [EMAIL_1], [PHONE_1]
- Stratocore DR lead: [PERSON_3], [EMAIL_3], [PHONE_3]

Company names (Stratocore, OpsVault) and contract terms are kept on purpose, so questions like "the cloud vendor's MSA" still work. The rule is: redact people and secrets, not companies.

4. Pain point → pipeline step

Each step exists because of a specific failure in the reviews above. Later posts explain each step in depth; here is why each one is in the pipeline.

StepNamePain it solves at OpsVault
Phase 1: Ingestion & Indexing (offline)
S1Data SourcesDecide what counts as evidence: 11 linked docs (MSAs, SOWs, NDAs, SOC 2 reports, policies, a questionnaire). Done.
S2Document LoadingTurn PDFs into clean text, so tables of control IDs and clause numbers survive.
S3, S14Chunking (clause-aware)Never split "§12.2 Data breach super cap" from its dollar figure.
S4Metadata ExtractionTag doc type, vendor, parties, dates and framework, so "Stratocore's SOWs" filters correctly and SOWs link to MSA-2025-014.
S5Security & PII SanitizationPresidio plus regex replace names, emails, bank numbers and keys with [PERSON_1]-style placeholders before embedding. A redaction log records the entity type and count, never the original value.
S6EmbeddingsOne switchable provider (OpenAI or Azure), reused later by query rewriting, generation and evals.
S7Vector DatabaseA cached FAISS index per use case, so docs aren't re-embedded on every run.
Phase 2: Pre-retrieval
S8Query Rewriting (+ query redaction)Turn "does the cloud guy rotate keys?" into a precise search, and strip any PII the user pasted in.
Phase 3: Retrieval
S9Hybrid Search (BM25 + vector)Vectors miss exact tokens. BM25 catches "CC6.1", "SC-ENC-02", "Section 12.2" and "$1,680,000".
Phase 4: Post-retrieval
S10RerankingA cross-encoder puts the exact clause first, ahead of chunks that only look similar.
S11Context AssemblyLabel each chunk [Source n] with its doc and section, so every claim can be traced.
Phase 5: Generation
S12LLM Generation (+ answer redaction)Grounded answers with citations, and "I don't know" when the docs are silent. The final answer is checked for PII once more and carries a "not legal advice" line.
Phase 6: Evaluation & Observability
S13EvaluationAnswer-key checks and an LLM judge for faithfulness. Would the system have caught the Cortexa §5.4 loophole?
S15Advanced Eval & ObservabilityLangSmith tracing, token and cost tracking, and a SQLite usage log. That gives auditors a record of who asked what and what it cost.
Phase 7: Serving
S16Web InterfacesA FastAPI /ask endpoint plus Gradio and Streamlit demos, so procurement and sales can use it without a terminal.
Phase 8: Agentic RAG & Production Hardening
S17Agentic RAG (LangGraph)A multi-step vendor review with tools: search_contracts, search_audit_reports, check_policy and create_risk_ticket (through a mock Table API). A human approves before any ticket is filed.
S18Prompt-Injection DefenseVendor documents are written by the vendor, so they're treated as untrusted data, not instructions.
S19GuardrailsOff-topic and "not legal advice" checks.
S20CI Eval GateTests and evals run on every push, so a prompt change can't silently break answer quality.
S21API Auth & RBACSales sees outbound answers; only GRC and legal see full vendor contracts.

5. Enterprise SaaS and PaaS Appeal

If you run AI or product engineering at an enterprise SaaS or PaaS company, these are the design choices that matter:

  • It starts from a buyer's problem, not a model. Vendor risk and security questionnaires already have owners (GRC, procurement, security, sales), budgets and deadlines. Each answer speeds up a vendor onboarding or a deal.
  • Governance is built in from the start. PII is redacted at three layers (ingestion, query and answer). Every answer is cited, there's a redaction audit log, and prompt-injection defense and RBAC are on the roadmap. That is what a CISO asks about first.
  • Evals come before features. Faithfulness checks and a CI gate on the roadmap (S13, S15, S20) mean that quality is measured, not demoed.
  • Agents keep a human in the loop. The S17 agent files risk tickets, but only after a person approves. That is the right default for anything that touches a risk register.
  • One pipeline serves many use cases. The pipeline code is business-agnostic. Metadata fields, PII rules, prompts and eval questions live in a use-cases/*.md file. Contract review for legal or policy Q&A for HR is a new use-case file, not a rewrite.
  • The path to product is Customer Zero. Prove it on your own vendors first, then sell it as a skill inside an existing product line.
